# Example — `startup-roast`

A real run of the `startup-roast` skill on a generic B2B landing page.

---

## Input

> Landing page for "FlowSync — the all-in-one platform for modern teams."
> Hero headline: "The all-in-one platform for modern teams." Subhead:
> "Streamline your workflow and unlock your team's potential." CTA: "Get Started."
> Target customer: ops leads at 50–200 person companies. Product actually does:
> automatically keeps every team's calendars in sync without manual invites.

---

## Output

# Startup Roast — FlowSync

## The 5-Second Test
> **What I thought this was:** Some kind of project management tool? Maybe a
> Notion competitor? Genuinely couldn't tell.
> **What it actually is:** A calendar-sync product.
> **Verdict: FAIL.** Five seconds in, I have no idea what you do. That's the
> whole ballgame, and right now you're losing it.

## Clarity 🔥
- **Headline:** "The all-in-one platform for modern teams" — this exact sentence
  has appeared on roughly ten thousand landing pages and accurately described
  none of them. It is content-free. *Fix:* say the literal thing the product
  does — "Keep every team's calendars in sync. No more manual invites."
- **Subhead:** "Streamline your workflow and unlock your team's potential" —
  more words, still zero information. *Fix:* "FlowSync watches your calendars and
  auto-syncs shared events across teams, so nobody double-books or misses a
  meeting." Boring? Maybe. Clear? Yes. Clear wins.
- **CTA:** "Get Started" — generic, but acceptable. Leave it; you have bigger
  problems.

## Messaging & Differentiation 🔥
- **Sentences that could be on any competitor's site, unchanged:** "all-in-one
  platform", "modern teams", "streamline your workflow", "unlock your potential".
  That's your entire hero. All of it is replaceable. All of it is dead weight.
- **What's actually different:** You do *one specific thing* — automatic
  cross-team calendar sync — and you're hiding it. The specificity is your
  advantage. Lead with it.
- **Fix:** Cut every abstract noun. Describe the one concrete thing. Specificity
  is more persuasive than ambition.

## UX & Conversion Path 🔥
- **Friction points:** (1) The hero gives the visitor no reason to scroll. (2)
  There's no product visual above the fold — you have a demoable product and
  you're describing it in adjectives instead. (3) Two CTAs ("Get Started" and
  "Learn More") split intent.
- **Fix:** Put the demo GIF above the fold. One CTA. Let the product do the talking.

## Trust Audit 🔥
- **Credibility gaps:** No logos, no testimonials, no numbers, no specifics.
  Nothing tells a stranger that a real company uses this. The page reads like a
  template because, messaging-wise, it is one.
- **Fix:** Even one real customer quote with a name and a company beats the
  entire current hero.

## Pricing 🔥
- Not reviewed — no pricing page provided.

## PMF Signal Read
There's a real product here doing a real, narrow, painful job (double-booking
across teams *is* a genuine ops headache). The PMF signal is buried under
generic marketing. The product might be fine; the page is the problem.

## The Verdict
- **Grade: D**
- **The #1 problem:** Nobody knows what you do within 5 seconds. Nothing else on
  this page matters until that's fixed.
- **Fix list (do in this order):**
  1. Rewrite the headline to say the literal thing the product does.
  2. Put the demo GIF above the fold.
  3. Cut every abstract sentence from the hero.
  4. Add one real customer proof point.
  5. Collapse to a single CTA.
- **What's already working:** Your demo GIF (currently buried at the bottom) is
  genuinely good — it shows the value in 3 seconds. It's doing the job your
  headline should be doing. Move it up.
