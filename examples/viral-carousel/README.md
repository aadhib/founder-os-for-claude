# Example — `viral-carousel`

A real run of the `viral-carousel` skill — an 8-slide LinkedIn carousel.

---

## Input

> Topic: "we cut churn from 8% to 3%". Platform: linkedin. Audience: SaaS founders.
> Proof: the fix was an onboarding change, not a retention campaign.

---

## Output

# Viral Carousel — Cutting churn from 8% to 3%

## Angle & Takeaway
- **Angle:** Churn wasn't a retention problem — it was an onboarding problem
  wearing a retention costume.
- **One-sentence takeaway:** If users churn, ask when they *stopped*, not why
  they *left* — the answer is usually "they never started."
- **Platform:** linkedin (1080×1080 square)

## Hook Options
1. "We cut churn from 8% to 3%. It took 3 days — and had nothing to do with retention."
2. "Everyone told us to fix retention. The problem was somewhere else entirely."
3. "Our churn was 8%. We ran the churn interviews. Every single one said the same 6 words."
4. "We almost built a retention team. Good thing we read the churn interviews first."
5. "8% → 3% churn. Zero retention campaigns. Here's what actually moved it."

**Chosen:** #1 — pairs a specific number with a misdirection ("nothing to do with
retention"), which creates an information gap the reader needs closed.

## Slides
| # | Slide copy | Purpose | Swipe trigger |
|---|------------|---------|---------------|
| 1 | **We cut churn from 8% to 3%.** It took 3 days — and had nothing to do with retention. | Stop the scroll | "how?" |

## Caption
We cut churn from 8% to 3% — and it had nothing to do with retention.

We were *this close* to building a whole retention function. Win-back emails,
loyalty features, the works.

Then we read the churn interviews. Every one said the same thing: "I never
really got it set up."

It was an onboarding problem all along. It just showed up 4 weeks later wearing a
churn costume.

The fix took 3 days. The retention team we never hired would have taken 3 months
and fixed nothing.

If users churn, ask *when they stopped* — not *why they left*.

Building in public — follow for the next teardown.

## Visual Direction
- **System:** near-black background `#0b0b0c`; one lime accent `#bef264`; one
  bold geometric sans (e.g. Inter / Geist) at two weights; generous margins;
  slide number bottom-right in dim gray.
- **Per-slide layout:** Slide 1 — the "8% → 3%" set huge, accent on "3%". Slide
  4 — the quote centered, large, in accent, everything else dim (this is the
  emotional peak). Slide 7 — the number "8% → 3%" again as a callback. Slide 8 —
  takeaway big, CTA small beneath.
- **Claude Design prompt:** "An 8-slide LinkedIn carousel, 1080×1080. Near-black
  background #0b0b0c, one lime accent #bef264, bold sans-serif, lots of negative
  space, slide numbers bottom-right. Minimal, premium, founder-content aesthetic
  — think a sharp solo operator, not a corporate deck. Slide 4 is the emotional
  peak: a single centered quote in the lime accent."

## Animation Ideas (video variant)
- Slide 1: the "8%" cross-fades into "3%" over 600ms ease-out.
- Slide 4: the quote types in character-by-character.
- Between slides: a fast 150ms horizontal wipe to mimic the swipe.
